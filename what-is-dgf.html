What Is the DGF?

Last updated: September 2026

Definition What governance means What the DGF covers Administration How to verify What it is not Sources

Short definition

The DGF is the set of rules and processes used to govern the WIN Blockchain. It is made up of Win Node Owners who can vote on proposals under a published charter. The goal is collective, transparent decision-making rather than one central party changing the network alone.

Think of it like a constitution for the network: who can vote, what can be decided, and where decisions are recorded.

What "governance" means here

Governance is simply how decisions get made.

Some blockchains are run like a company: a small team decides. Distributed governance tries to spread that power across node owners, with public rules meant to protect the network while still allowing it to evolve.

Connect support materials describe the WIN Blockchain as governed by the Win DGF — a blockchain governance coordination protocol composed of Win Node Owners.

What the DGF is responsible for

According to Connect's DGF overview and the WIN Charter materials, the DGF framework relates to governance of areas such as:

  • The WIN Blockchain itself
  • Features of the distribution / reward parameters
  • Shared infrastructure and common resources
  • Processes for proposals and voting by eligible node owners

Node licenses include rights and responsibilities inside the DGF — including voting rights on governance proposals.

Who supports administration?

Connect's DGF article notes that administration of blockchain governance is supported by Node Governance, a Swiss entity delegated certain authority under the DGF charter to operate on behalf of the Win Node Network (for example, responsibilities related to listings and operational duties assigned under that charter).

That does not replace reading the charter yourself. It means governance has both community voting and named administrative support — separate ideas that should be checked in the primary documents.

How to verify DGF claims yourself

  1. Open the public DGF site: win.node-governance.io
  2. Read the Charter PDF: win-charter.pdf
  3. Look for voter eligibility, proposal scope, and decision records
  4. For a hands-on skill, see Connect's vote-audit walkthrough on Medium

If a project says "community governed" but you cannot find a charter, voter definition, or public decisions, treat that as a red flag — on any chain, not just this one.

What the DGF is not

  • It is not a guarantee that every participant will like every vote outcome
  • It is not the same thing as the Connect company website
  • It is not financial advice, and governance votes can change reward parameters
